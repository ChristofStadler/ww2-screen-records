Key to gradings

A2Strongly recommended. Sound history, or great filmmaking, usually both.
B1Very good, with some dramatic licence taken knowingly.
B2Worth your evening. Treat the detail loosely.
C3Entertainment first. Enjoyable, but do not learn your history here.

Remarks by the assessing officer

  • On sequence. Titles sit under the period they portray, not the year they were made. A 1957 picture about 1944 is filed under 1944. Where a work runs the length of the war it is placed in Part X.
  • On the Eastern Front. Roughly four in five German soldiers who died in this war died fighting the Soviets. Anglo-American cinema barely registers this. Parts IV to VIII are weighted accordingly — Come and See, The Ascent and Ivan's Childhood are the corrective.
  • On the enemy's own films. The German and Japanese entries here are post-war reckonings, not wartime propaganda. Die Brücke, Der Untergang and The Human Condition are among the harshest films any nation has made about itself.
  • On the flagged entries. A CAVEAT mark means the picture is enjoyable but materially misleads on a point of record. It is not a bar to watching — only to citing.
  • Suggested first six. Come and See · Das Boot · Grave of the Fireflies · Army of Shadows · Downfall · Band of Brothers. Between them: the partisan war, the Atlantic, the firebombing of Japan, the Resistance, the collapse of the Reich, and the rifle company.
